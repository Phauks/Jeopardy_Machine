### Picture rounds
- **"Name That Park"** (owner's remembered category): NPS photos are public domain as federal works (credit "National Park Service"; beware occasional third-party images — check each asset's credit line). Sources: **NPGallery** (npgallery.nps.gov — filter by park; "Public Domain, High Resolution" gallery at nps.gov/media/photo), **Wikimedia Commons** category "National Parks of the United States." Good format: iconic-but-not-labeled vistas (Delicate Arch, Half Dome, Old Faithful, Crater Lake's Wizard Island, White Sands dunes).
- **"Name This Endangered Species"**: **USFWS National Digital Library** (digitalmedia.fws.gov — US federal, public domain) for bald eagle, California condor, black-footed ferret, whooping crane; Wikimedia Commons for vaquita (illustrations — almost no photos exist, itself a fun talking point), kākāpō (NZ DOC images on Commons are CC — check individual license), axolotl.
- **"Name That Box / Zoomed-In Game Pieces"**: photograph the clubs' own physical games (box art close-ups, a single Wingspan egg, a Catan robber, a Cascadia hex). Photographing your own copies for a one-off private club event is the practical, low-risk approach; official press-kit assets (e.g., Stonemaier's press page) are another.
- **Video game screenshots**: fair-use gray zone; safest is short original-capture screenshots for a private, noncommercial club night, or publisher press kits (most indie eco-games — Terra Nil, Alba, Endling — have public press kits with approved screenshots).
- **NASA media** for "Games in Spaaace" (Mir station photos, Soyuz launches): NASA content is generally not copyrighted; credit NASA; don't use the insignia/logo. Source: **images.nasa.gov**.

### Audio rounds
- **Bird/animal calls**: **Xeno-canto** (xeno-canto.org) — 1M+ recordings, all Creative Commons; filter for CC-BY/CC0 and credit the recordist. **Macaulay Library** (Cornell) requires permission — prefer Xeno-canto. Fun picks: common loon, kākāpō "boom," barred owl ("who cooks for you"), sandhill crane. **Freesound.org** — CC0/CC-BY sound effects (filter by license).
- **Game music — copyright caution**: commercial VGM (Mario, Zelda, Minecraft) is copyrighted; playing it at a private club night is low-risk in practice but technically requires a license, and streaming/recording the event would risk takedowns. Safe approaches: (a) chiptune covers released CC (check ocremix.org terms), (b) 8-bit style CC0 tracks from Freesound/opengameart.org played as "name the STYLE/era", (c) hum-it-yourself rounds, (d) public-domain classical pieces famously used in games. Great trivia hook: "Tetris's theme is actually this 19th-century Russian folk song" → *What is Korobeiniki?* — the FOLK SONG is public domain; specific recordings are not. Verify details before use.
- **Video clips**: NPS B-roll on NPGallery (public domain); NASA video (images.nasa.gov); publisher game trailers (press kits generally permit event use).

## 6. Fact-check flags (avoid these traps)

1. **"Pong was the first video game"** — false; Pong (1972) was the first blockbuster. "First" depends on definition: Cathode-Ray Tube Amusement Device (1948 patent), Bertie the Brain (1950), OXO (1952), Tennis for Two (1958), Spacewar! (1962). Phrase clues as "first commercially successful" or name the specific milestone.
2. **"Charles Darrow invented Monopoly"** — myth; it's Lizzie Magie's Landlord's Game (1904). Use the myth AS the clue, never as the answer.
3. **"Great Wall of China visible from space/Moon"** — false per NASA and Apollo astronauts. Only use as a myth-busting clue.
4. **"Pac-Man was inspired by a pizza"** — Iwatani himself calls it a "half-truth"; the kanji 口 (mouth) was equally the source. Don't state the pizza story as plain fact.
